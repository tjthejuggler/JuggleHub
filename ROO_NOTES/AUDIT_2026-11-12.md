# Audit of the tracking project — 2026-11-12

Scope: everything described in [`HANDOFF_STATE_OF_THE_PROJECT.md`](ROO_NOTES/HANDOFF_STATE_OF_THE_PROJECT.md) (the `tracking_lab/` work of 2026-11-10 and 11). No camera was attached during the audit, so every finding comes from the saved recordings, datasets and code. Nothing was trained.

**If you are taking over this work, read [`ROO_NOTES/TAKEOVER_NOTES_2026-11-12.md`](ROO_NOTES/TAKEOVER_NOTES_2026-11-12.md) next.** It lists every remaining task with exact steps.

Each statement below is one of three kinds, and says which:
- **Measured**: computed during the audit from files on disk; the scripts and outputs are in `.tmp/audit/`.
- **Inferred**: the most likely explanation of something measured. Could be wrong.
- **Not tested**: written but never run in the situation it is meant for.

## 1. The five findings that change the plan

### 1.1 The two LED recordings are 5 fps recordings. Every LED and exposure conclusion is withdrawn.

Measured:

| RGB exposure value passed | labelled | unique colour frames per second | each image repeated |
|---|---|---|---|
| auto | — | 30 | 1× |
| 2000 | "2 ms" | 5.1 | 6× |
| 4000 | "4 ms" | 2.6 | 12× |
| 8000 | "8 ms" | 1.3 | 24× |

- `2026-11-11_15-06-00_cascade3_glow_same`: 301 unique colour frames of 1,799.
- `2026-11-11_15-49-52_cascade3_glow_mixed_locked`: 300 unique of 1,801. This one was described as "auto exposure restored".
- The other 12 recordings are clean. Depth stayed at 30 fps in the broken ones, and `meta.json`'s `effective_fps` reads about 30 because it measures the loop, so nothing flagged it.

Inferred: one unit of the RealSense RGB exposure option is 100 µs, not 1 µs, so "2 ms" was a 200 ms exposure. The frame periods fit that exactly. It has not been checked on the camera. Also inferred: the second session inherited the manual setting from an earlier run (or the lock variable was still set).

What this withdraws: "the 2 ms lock is catastrophic for LED balls", "LED balls streak at any exposure", the "58% LED gap" (125 of 216), the "2ms_g16 winner" of the exposure test, the "400 µs / gain 64 optimum", and the "+66% full-ball frames for matte balls". All were measured on repeated, long-exposure frames. **Nothing is currently known about LED balls at a real 30 fps.**

### 1.2 The V12 dataset that was about to be uploaded was broken.

Measured on `.tmp/v12_dataset`:
- Its yaml declared 1 class; its labels contain class ids 0 and 1. Ultralytics drops such images: 15,310 of 22,969 train and 3,677 of 4,856 val.
- 10,656 of the 27,825 images were unique. The "five V8-era training sets" are successive generations of the same frames.
- 2,783 val files were byte-identical to a train file. 7,117 images carried two different label versions.
- 240 frames came from the six regression videos, and 80 from the two broken LED sessions.

### 1.3 The six-video suite is a tuning set, and there is nothing else.

Measured, V8 detector, code as it stands, identical on every run:

| session | counted | catches | throws | catches backed by a flight | same-frame transfers | frames with 2 balls in one hand |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cascade60 | 60 | 63 | 62 | 54 | 6 | 37.5% |
| box30 | 30 | 26 | 23 | 13 | 10 | 35.2% |
| 441_30 | 30 | 24 | 21 | 17 | 4 | 30.4% |
| shower40 | 40 | 38 | 37 | 18 | 16 | 44.3% |
| columns30 | 30 | 28 | 26 | 20 | 5 | 22.2% |
| flash_10 | 30 | 28 | 27 | 19 | 5 | 14.5% |

- Mean |catch error| is **3.17** (the handoff said ~2.2). Mean |throw error| is 4.67. Throws are under-counted in five of the six.
- 68% of catches follow an observed flight of the same ball. The other 32% are produced by heuristics, mostly the same-frame "transfer" THROW+CATCH pair.
- Two balls are HELD in the same hand in 14–44% of frames over whole clips. A juggler does hold two balls in one hand before the first throw and after the last catch, so the fairer figure counts only from the first THROW to the last CATCH: 18.5–49.4% (measured, `.tmp/audit/double_held_window.py`). Inside the run that cannot happen, so the track states are often wrong even where the total is close.
- Every constant was chosen on these six clips. The sweep shows how tightly:

| constant | values tried → mean catch error (current value in bold) |
|---|---|
| held_radius_px | 45→8.33, 55→12.33, 65→6.50, **75→3.17**, 85→3.00, 95→3.83, 110→7.00 |
| held_dwell | 1→3.50, **2→3.17**, 3→7.17, 4→12.83 |
| assoc_gate_px | 60→10.00, 70→4.17, **80→3.17**, 90→2.83, 110→2.83 |
| max_catch_speed | 300→7.67, 375→3.00, **450→3.17**, 525→2.67, 600→2.33, 800→2.83 |
| throw_dwell | 2→3.67, **3→3.17**, 4→3.00, 5→3.50 |
| gravity_px_s2 | 1500→3.33, 2000→3.50, **2500→3.17**, 3000→3.00, 3500→2.67 |
| min_catch_gap | 0→3.17, 2→3.00, **4→3.17**, 6→3.00, 8→3.00 |
| min_flight_frames | 1→3.17, **2→3.17**, 3→3.17, 4→3.50 |
| min_hits | 2→3.17, **3→3.17**, 4→3.67 |
| held_max_occluded | 6, 8, **10**, 12, 15 → 3.17 for all |

A constant whose neighbours double the error is fitted to the clips. No constant was changed: picking the slightly better values (for example max_catch_speed 600) would be more of the same fitting. Differences under about 1.0 are also inside the ±1–2 uncertainty of the hand counts.

With both LED sessions invalid, **there is no valid recording that the tracker was not tuned on.** How it behaves on a new take is unknown.

### 1.4 V11 was trained on the videos it was scored on.

Measured: 240 of V11's 559 training frames come from the six regression videos (40 each); all 14 source recordings appear in both its train and val split. Its suite result is 59/24/27/29/39/30, mean error **5.0** (the docs said ~3.3; the same counts give 5.0). Its labels came from a vision API; 39% of those boxes overlap a V8 detection at IoU ≥ 0.5, which suggests they are noisy (V8 is not ground truth, so this is indicative only).

### 1.5 The pipeline is not real-time.

Measured offline on this machine: pose 23.9 ms + ball 21.1 ms + MOG2 6.8 ms + histogram 0.4 ms = 52.3 ms per frame, 19 fps, against a 30 fps camera. There is no live runner; everything so far is offline replay.

### Smaller corrections
- The deployed "V8" model has 2 classes (ball, ball_held), not 1. The tracker ignores the class.
- V8 was most likely trained on `V8_held_balls` (inferred from matching file dates, 2025-10-02). No V9.2, V9.3 or V10 model exists on disk.
- `held_radius` is 75 px in the code, not 45.
- Earlier val mAP figures came from frame-level splits (in `V9_3_targeted`, all 88 val recordings are also in train), so they are inflated.
- An ngrok token sat in plaintext in `colab_setup_cell.py`, and the handoff doc held a live tunnel address and root password. Neither was ever committed to git.

## 3. Next steps, in order

The reasoning behind the order: at the moment no change to the detector, the tracker or the camera settings can be judged, because the only valid counted data is the data everything was fitted to. So the first three steps produce data, and only then does it make sense to change things.

### Step 1 — Confirm the camera (about 5 minutes, needs the D455)

1. Run `python3 -m tracking_lab.wizard`. The terminal prints two lines: `RGB exposure: AUTO` and `RGB exposure_range_units: [min, max, step, default]`. Keep the second line.
2. Look at the live view for ten seconds while moving. The top of the picture should say `exposure: AUTO` and there should be **no** red `BAD STREAM` line. Press `q`.
3. Run `TRACKING_EXPOSURE_MS=20 python3 -m tracking_lab.wizard` and look again.
   - Normally exposed or bright picture, no BAD STREAM line: the unit is 100 µs, as assumed. Done.
   - Nearly black picture: the unit is 1 µs. Change `RGB_EXPOSURE_UNIT_MS` in `camera.py` from `0.1` to `0.001`. In that case the cause of the 5 fps recordings is something else and needs a fresh look before any manual exposure is used.
   - BAD STREAM line: the conversion is wrong in the other direction. Stop and report the printed lines.
4. Run `python3 -m tracking_lab.wizard` once more without the variable. It must say AUTO again with no warning. This checks that a manual setting does not stick.

If any script fails on the device, that is expected to be a small fix: none of the camera code has run on hardware yet.

### Step 2 — Record held-out counted takes (about 10 minutes of juggling)

1. Ordinary balls, same room and framing as before: `python3 -m tracking_lab.verify_wizard --heldout h1`. It asks for the same six patterns and counts as last time. Stop at the count, press SPACE. Press `R` if you drop or lose count.
2. LED balls: `python3 -m tracking_lab.verify_wizard --heldout led1 --only cascade60`. One 60-catch cascade at auto exposure is enough to find out whether LED balls are a problem at all.
3. Watch for the red BAD STREAM line while recording. A take that ends with the terminal warning about repeated frames is unusable; redo it.
4. Run `python3 -m tracking_lab.evaluate`. The HELD-OUT table is the first honest number the project has.

Rules that keep it honest:
- Never choose a constant or a model because it improves the held-out table. Use it to decide between ideas, then record a fresh set (`--heldout h2`) to confirm.
- Never put a counted take into a training set. `build_v12_dataset.py` already refuses them. Do not re-run `build_v11_dataset.py`; it samples every recording.

How to read the result:
- Held-out error close to the tuning error (about 3 per take): the constants generalise. Go on to step 5 and aim at the structural problems.
- Held-out error much larger: the constants are overfitted. Step 5 becomes urgent and should replace thresholds with rules that have no threshold (see below) before any detector work.
- LED take close to the others: the LED problem was the recording, and the LED-specific ideas (streak endpoints, special exposure) can be dropped. LED take much worse: do step 7.

### Step 3 — Per-event ground truth for two or three takes (not built yet)

A total hides which catches were missed and which were invented. What is needed, for cascade and box at least, is a list of (frame, hand) for every real catch.

- Proposed tool: extend [`tracking_lab/counter.py`](tracking_lab/counter.py). It already plays a take frame by frame and has a `C` key that adds one to a total. Make `C` also store the current frame number, add `L`/`R` for the hand, and save the list beside the count.
- Proposed scorer: in `evaluate.py`, match tracker catches to true catches within ±4 frames and the same hand. Report missed, phantom and wrong-hand catches separately.
- Cost to the user: roughly 10 minutes per take with frame stepping.

This turns "26 of 30" into "5 missed, 1 phantom", which is what tells you what to fix.

### Step 4 — Decide about the detector with evidence

Do not train yet. First find out whether detection is the limiting factor:
- Measured: on the six clips the fused detector returns at least 3 candidates in 84% of frames (3 in 64.0%, 4 or more in 20.1%, fewer in 15.9%).
- Hand-label about 150 frames from the new held-out takes (25 per take, evenly spaced) with the annotation process used for the earlier datasets (see `Notes/tier1_dataset_creation/How_To_Annotate.md`; the audit did not review it). That is the first detection test set from the footage the tracker is judged on.
- Score V8 on it. If recall is high, leave the detector alone and work on the tracker.

If training is justified:
1. `python3 -m tracking_lab.build_v12_dataset --check` (re-verifies `.tmp/v12_clean`), then `cd .tmp && zip -q -r v12_clean.zip v12_clean` (the folder is 441 MB and JPEGs barely compress, so expect a zip of similar size).
2. Upload to `/content/` on Colab, run [`colab_train_v12_cell.py`](tracking_lab/colab_train_v12_cell.py).
3. Download the exported OpenVINO folder and run `TRACKING_BALL_MODEL=<path>/best.xml python3 -m tracking_lab.evaluate`.
4. Keep the new model only if it is better on the hand-labelled test set **and** not worse on the held-out takes.

Expectations: `v12_clean` is essentially the data V8 saw (4,990 frames against 4,476, same boxes resized by a median of 2 px, and 887 of them held back for validation). A large gain is unlikely. Its val mAP will read lower than the old figures because the split is finally by recording. `--with-glm` adds 239 machine-labelled frames from the uncounted November recordings; try it as a second run, not the first.

### Step 5 — Tracker structure (the best-supported improvement)

Three measured defects, each with a rule that needs no tuned threshold:
1. **Two balls in one hand** (14–44% of frames). When two tracks are HELD by the same hand, at most one can be right. Resolve it at the moment the second one enters: the one with a detection keeps the hand, the other is released.
2. **Unbacked catches** (32%). Most come from the same-frame transfer pair. Check each against step 3's ground truth: if they are real hand-to-hand passes, keep the rule; if they are phantoms that happen to cancel misses, remove it and fix the misses.
3. **Throws under-counted** (mean error 4.67 against 3.17 for catches; catches exceed throws in all six). A ball cannot be caught twice without a throw between. Emit the missing throw when a held track is next seen in flight, instead of the current silent release.

Judge each one on: the diagnostics (2held%, flight-backed share, catches − throws), the per-event misses and phantoms from step 3, and the held-out table. Revert anything that is worse on held-out.

Deferred until the above is done: Kalman filter, tempo-adaptive gating, ID stitching. None has evidence behind it yet.

### Step 6 — Real time

Target: under 33 ms per frame end to end, then a live runner. Options, none measured yet:
- Run pose every second or third frame and interpolate wrists (pose is 23.9 ms, the largest item).
- 416-px models exist for both ball and pose (`engine/models/zipped/V8_balls_held416`, `yolo11n-pose416`). Measured for the ball model only: swapping it in with the constants unchanged raises the mean catch error on the six tuning videos from 3.17 to 8.83 (`.tmp/audit/eval_v8_416.txt`), so it is not a drop-in replacement. Its speed and the 416 pose model are not measured.
- Run the two models in parallel with OpenVINO async requests; the machine has 22 logical cores.
- OpenVINO lists a GPU device. The handoff says the iGPU was unusable in earlier sessions; it is worth one 10-minute measurement with these small models.
- Then write the live runner (camera → perception → tracker → on-screen counts), with the repeated-frame guard included. Measure latency on the device, not offline.

### Step 7 — LED exposure, only if step 2 shows a problem

Once the unit is confirmed, `python3 -m tracking_lab.exposure_test` sweeps real 16 / 8 / 4 / 2 ms settings and reports unique frames per second next to detections; settings with repeated frames are disqualified. Its gains are untested starting points.
